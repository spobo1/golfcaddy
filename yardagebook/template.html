Yardages are straight lines from each tee box to the centre of the green, with the front and back of the green underneath, so doglegs read shorter than the scorecard. Front and back are where the line of approach into the green crosses its edge. Hazard yardages are from the back tee: reach is the near edge, carry the far edge. Sides are as seen from the tee. OpenStreetMap doesn't link hazards to holes, so each is matched to the hole that passes closest to it; water near two holes is listed on both. Tees are listed from the back.

Caddy suggestions use typical distances for the chosen skill level, assuming a full bag from driver to lob wedge; a player's own shot history would replace them. From the chosen tee it takes, of the clubs that won't land in or roll into a bunker or water in the way, the one finishing closest to the green centre, counting yards past the back of the green double. The driver is only suggested when the green is out of reach of every other club. It doesn't allow for wind, elevation or lie.
